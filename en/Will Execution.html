Will Execution & Estate Administration Handbook

Congratulations on making the prudent decision to prepare a will.

A will helps you distribute your legitimate personal assets according to your wishes. However, missing information or inadequate documentation can prolong estate administration and hinder your family's inheritance process.

Compiled under the Taiwan Civil Code (Part V: Succession) and the Estate and Gift Tax Act, this handbook guides executors and heirs through the will execution and inheritance workflow.

Organize and safeguard all essential documents in advance, clarifying the necessary procedures for your family and ensuring your legacy is fulfilled smoothly.

Statutory Duties of the Executor

Under Article 1215 of the Taiwan Civil Code, an executor has the duty to manage the estate and perform all acts necessary to carry out the will upon succession.

  • Prepare an estate inventory and deliver it to all legal heirs (Civil Code Art. 1214)
  • File the estate tax return with the National Taxation Bureau within 6 months of death
  • Take necessary measures to prevent estate assets from being damaged or lost
  • Collect claims and settle the decedent's debts and outstanding tax liabilities
  • Distribute legacies and partition the estate subject to statutory Compulsory Portion rules
  • Perform all other necessary administrative actions related to estate transfers and management

Required Documents

We recommend retaining copies of the following documents in a safe place.
Note down the location in your asset ledger and inform your executor.

Personal & Kinship Documents

Used to verify identity, familial relationships, and marital status—essential core documents for inheritance.

5 Documents
  • Official Death Certificate or Autopsy Verification Certificate
  • De-registered Household Registration Transcript of the Deceased
  • Heirs' Current Household Transcripts, National IDs, and Seal Certificates
  • Inheritance Succession Tree (detailing legal kinship across statutory orders)
  • Original Will (Holographic, Notarial, Sealed, Dictated, or Oral)

Financial, Insurance & Tax Records

Bank accounts, Labor Pensions, social/commercial insurance, securities, and tax records.

6 Documents
  • Labor Pension individual account records and Labor Insurance claim files
  • Commercial life insurance policy documents and beneficiary designations
  • Securities depository accounts, TDCC passbooks, and mutual fund statements
  • Credit card, mortgage, and loan agreement contracts
  • Bank passbooks, certificates of deposit, and balance certificates
  • Original Estate Tax Payment Certificate (or Exemption Certificate) from Taxation Bureau

Real Estate, Business & Other Asset Records

Property deeds, vehicles, corporate shares, and valuable property certificates.

5 Documents
  • Vehicle license and original registration inspection documents
  • Land and Building Title Deeds, property sale contracts, mortgage records
  • Company registration transcripts, shareholder register, articles of incorporation
  • Safe deposit box lease contract, keys, and branch location details
  • Local tax clearance certificates (Land Value & House Tax no-arrears verification)

How to Handle Different Asset Classes

Understand statutory guidelines, required documents, and practical procedures under Taiwan jurisdiction.

Bank Deposits

Account inquiry, withdrawal, and title transfer

Basic Documentation

  • Official Death Certificate / De-registered Household Transcript
  • Bank passbooks, account information, and deposit certificates

Procedures for Heirs / Executors

Heirs can apply for the National Taxation Bureau's "Single Window Financial Asset Inquiry" to check all balances. Except for small balances qualifying for expedited closure under internal bank limits, withdrawals require the Estate Tax Clearance Certificate and formal inheritance documents.

Standard Required Submissions

  • Original Will (if applicable)
  • Death Certificate & De-registered Household Transcript
  • Heirs' IDs, Household Transcripts, and Seal Certificates
  • National Taxation Bureau Estate Tax Payment (or Exemption) Certificate

Safe Deposit Boxes

Opening box and asset inventory

Mandatory Statutory Procedures

Under Article 40 of Taiwan's Estate and Gift Tax Act, before opening a safe deposit box leased by the decedent, the executor or heirs must notify the National Taxation Bureau to dispatch an official to conduct a joint on-site inspection and prepare a formal inventory list before releasing contents.

Labor Pensions & Social Insurance

Labor pension individual accounts and Labor Insurance death benefits

Labor Pension Individual Accounts (New Pension System)

Unclaimed pension balances in the decedent's Labor Pension personal account constitute part of the estate. Eligible designated survivors may claim the funds from the Bureau of Labor Insurance, and the amount must be reported on the estate tax return.

Labor Insurance Death Benefits

Funeral grants and survivor annuities/allowances are social welfare benefits granted to eligible dependants under the Labor Insurance Act. They do not form part of the estate and are exempt from estate tax.

Commercial Insurance

Insurance claims and estate tax considerations

Policies with Designated Beneficiaries

Under Article 112 of the Taiwan Insurance Act, life insurance payouts to designated beneficiaries are not included in the estate. However, under the Taxpayer Rights Protection Act, tax authorities review for intentional tax avoidance (e.g., massive lump-sum payments during critical illness or advanced age) based on the substance-over-form principle.

Policies without Designated Beneficiaries

Proceeds from policies lacking a designated beneficiary become part of the decedent's estate, subject to estate tax and distribution via will or statutory inheritance.

Enterprises & Equity

Sole proprietorships, limited companies, and corporate shares

Sole Proprietorships & Partnerships

A sole proprietorship registration cannot be directly inherited as an entity, but its assets and net equity belong to the estate. Heirs must settle outstanding liabilities and apply for ownership change or dissolution with municipal commerce bureaus.

Limited Companies & Companies Limited by Shares

Heirs inherit capital contributions and shares pursuant to the Taiwan Company Act and the company's articles of incorporation. Executors provide death certificates, the will, and estate tax clearance certificates to process shareholder title changes and Ministry of Economic Affairs registration.

Securities, Funds & Investments

Stocks, mutual funds, and wealth management portfolios

Procedures for Executors & Heirs

Securities and funds form part of the estate. Heirs must submit the estate tax clearance certificate, will or partition agreement, and identity documents to the brokerage firm to complete book-entry transfers through the Taiwan Depository & Clearing Corporation (TDCC).

Motor Vehicles

Vehicle inheritance title transfer

Procedures for Heirs

Heirs must apply for an inheritance transfer at the Motor Vehicle Office within 1 year of death. Present the vehicle license, estate tax clearance certificate, will or inheritance agreement, and proof of Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance (valid for at least 30 days) after clearing vehicle taxes and outstanding fines.

Real Estate (Land & Buildings)

Land Office inheritance registration

Real estate includes residential properties, land parcels, commercial units, and building rights.

Statutory Deadline and Penalties

Under Article 73 of the Taiwan Land Act, application for inheritance registration must be made within 6 months from the date of succession to the local Land Office. Late applications incur monthly fines up to 20 times the statutory registration fee, and properties left unregistered for over 1 year may be put under public listing management.

Prior to registration, obtain tax clearance on Land Value Tax and House Tax from local revenue agencies, settle mortgage arrangements with banks, and submit the Estate Tax Clearance Certificate to complete registration.

Estate Tax & Compulsory Portions

Estate tax filing deadlines and legal reserve rights

Filing Window and Tax Clearance

Under the Estate and Gift Tax Act, taxpayers must file the estate tax return with the National Taxation Bureau within 6 months of death (extendable by 3 months upon timely application). All debts and taxes must be cleared using estate assets before any property is transferred or partitioned.

While a testator may freely allocate assets via will, the disposition must not infringe upon the statutory Compulsory Portion (特留分) of legal heirs as prescribed under Taiwan Civil Code Article 1223.
